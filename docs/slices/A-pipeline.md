# Slice A: Bundle pipeline

Owner: Claude session, branch `slice/a-pipeline` · Phase: MVP · Migrations 0100–0199

## Goal
After upload, the expert gets a numbered, sorted bundle: every page read by OCR, its source and dates identified, facts extracted with links to the exact words, duplicates set aside, gaps and missing documents listed, and a ready-made email to ask the instructing party (the solicitor, under gb-eaw) for what's missing.

## Screens
- `S30-NotThisPatient` Not this patient's record (canvas board 30)
- 07 Preparing your bundle (`S07-Preparing`)
- 08 Your bundle is ready (`S08-BundleReady`)
- 08b Ask the instructing party for what's missing (`S08b-AskSolicitor`; pack label "Ask the solicitor")

## Endpoints (architecture 5.2)
- `POST /v1/cases/{id}/process` → 202 job; `GET /v1/cases/{id}/process` → the latest processing job (screen 07 follows it)
- `GET /v1/cases/{id}/bundle/summary`
- `GET /v1/cases/{id}/duplicates`; `PUT` and `PATCH /v1/cases/{id}/pages/{page_id}/duplicate`
- `PATCH /v1/cases/{id}/documents/{doc_id}` (provenance)
- `PATCH /v1/cases/{id}/missing-items/{item_id}`
- `POST /v1/cases/{id}/instructing-party-requests/draft`; `POST /v1/cases/{id}/instructing-party-requests` (returns `mailto:` link in MVP) The draft body takes `{extension: bool}` and returns `extension {days, due_date, after_date}` (pack `request_extension`), used by the S08b "Need more time?" aside.
- `POST /v1/cases/{id}/documents/append` → pipeline on new pages only
- `GET /v1/cases/{id}/pages/{page_no}/thumbnail` (for duplicate review)

## Tables owned
`ocr_words`, `missing_items`, `record_gaps`, `record_entries`, `instructing_party_requests`. Writes the OCR/source/duplicate columns of `pages` and inserts `facts` with status `suggested` (contract owned by foundation).

## Worker job: `process_bundle` / `process_pages`
split (pikepdf) → render 150–200 dpi (pypdfium2) → hashes → text-layer check (born-digital pages: words from pypdfium2, no OCR) → **T-07a** OCR (Azure DI Layout) for the rest → low-confidence flag → `pii.strip` → **T-07b** extraction (Sonnet 5, cached prompt), up to `extraction_batch_size` pages (default 10) per call, to stay inside the cell's Bedrock on-demand throughput ceiling — the model answers each page under its own `page_no`, never citing another page's word IDs → `provenance.check` per page → date normalisation (dateparser, date order from the pack) → duplicates (pHash + MinHash + rapidfuzz, with date/value equality) → record gaps (monthly density per source) → **T-07c** missing documents (references matched to inventory) → embeddings (bge-small) → email via Resend ("Your bundle is ready", no patient details).

## AI tasks (architecture 6.3)
| Task | Model | Output | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-07a | Azure DI `prebuilt-layout` | words, polygons, confidence, handwriting | low confidence → screen 10 queue |
| T-07b | Claude Sonnet 5 (in-region) | one answer per page in `pages[]` (each carrying its own `page_no`): `source_type`, `printed_dates[]`, `facts[]`, `references_out[]`, `availability_evidence[]`, `actions_requested_of_clinician[]`, all with `word_ids` | value-in-span; any page's failure retries the whole batch once, then that page is `needs_human`; a page that passes but keeps little or nothing (`no_facts`/`low_coverage`) is asked once more on its own, keeping the second answer only if it keeps strictly more |
| T-07c | Sonnet 5 (candidate pages chosen by code, nearest by date then page number, until bge-small is in the cell) | `{reference_id, matched_page or null, confidence}` | unmatched, or a page the case lacks → missing list; expert confirms |

## Contracts
- **In:** foundation uploads, jobs, AI kit, `pages` and `facts` contracts.
- **Out:** `facts` (suggested, with `word_ids`, `bbox`, `event_date`, `availability_evidence`), page images and thumbnails, low-confidence queue (B), missing items (08b), appended-page processing (reused by G).

## Records transcript (ADR 0028)
- T-07b returns `entries[]` (kind, first and last word id, author role, specialty); stored in `record_entries` (0121, purge order 1015). Job `extract_entries` backfills pages read before this change. An entry that overlaps the one before it is trimmed to start after it (empty ones are dropped and counted). Entry dates: a birth date is never used, whether recorded or printed directly after a birth label (the pack's identifier recogniser says which text is a date of birth, so a case with no recorded date of birth is covered; the same date printed again elsewhere on the page is the birth date too); a recorded date of birth is matched on its printed day, month and the last two digits of the year; a two-digit year is read in the pack's date order, only by the repair job, in the latest century not after the whole bundle's latest full date and not before the date of birth when known (`printed_century`); extraction never reads it (the "latest so far" depends on page order), and when no century fits it still blocks the entry (`please_check`); then one distinct date is used, else the one on the entry's first printed line (not for the entry holding the page's first word, which holds the header), else `please_check`; a date taken only from the last printed line by an entry that reaches the page's end and has lines before it (a footer) is `please_check`. An entry holding three or more lines that start with different printed dates (a card with a line per visit; not the pack's letter kind; two dates are a result's sample and report dates) is split into one entry per dated line (`extract.split_at_dated_lines`: contiguous word ranges that cover the entry, no model call; two dated lines in a row with the same date stay together; a piece that would be only the page's first or last line stays with its neighbour), in extraction and in the repair job. The repair job (`repair_fact_dates`) dates entries that have none, dates again an entry whose date is a birth date, splits a dated card the same way, and takes the line directly above (`line_above`) only when the entry printed no date and the date lies in no entry of the page; an entry with a date of its own is otherwise never changed. A page with enough words whose entries cover under 80% of them is asked once more (`low_entry_coverage`). Duplicates: the picture hash is not asked when word similarity is at least 0.95, and separators between two digits (`/ . : - ` or a space; not a comma) do not make a changed number, in a date, a long identifier or a number whose groups all have two digits or more (`1/2` is not `1.2`, `10 5` is not `10.5`). Word similarity of 0.6 or more makes a likely pair (the word-by-word check stays strict); a header or footer one copy may lack is a run of 3 to 12 typed words with no digit that starts or ends at least three other pages of different content (`edge_runs` counts each distinct page once and leaves the page under test out); a line that is only moved is never a copy (its list or dated entry may be what it means). A page asked again for thin entries is counted by the cost guard like any call; when it trips the run pauses with nothing half-written. Every OCR call carries the pack locale. Slice B reads through `core/contracts/record_entries.py` (`entries_for_case`, `entries_ready`, `entries_status`, `request_entries`).
- Pages the model could not read: a batch answer that leaves pages out has just those pages asked again on their own (`asking.answer_group`), so one page costs only itself. One definition (`record_entries.retryable_unread_page_ids`) says which pages can be tried again: entries failed (whatever the label), words to read, not set aside, not copies. The job, `failed_pages` on the summary and the transcript button all use it. `POST /transcript/retry` (and the S08 button) tries the page retry first, else the entries job after it failed; it queues the worker job `retry_unread_pages` (0131, action `page.read`) once (advisory lock per case), which marks only those pages to be extracted again and rejects their unverified suggestions. Their failed marker stays until an extraction succeeds, so a retry that fails every attempt can be pressed again. Good pages and verified facts are never touched; nothing retries by itself. `GET /bundle/summary` carries `failed_pages` and `retrying` (pages failed and an entries or retry job waiting); S08 says "N pages weren't processed" with a "Try these pages again" button and polls while `retrying`. While a retry runs the transcript is `preparing` (not ready) with the words "trying the pages we couldn't read again"; its contents offers the button only to people with `page.read` and when `failed_pages > 0`.
- Retry progress: `GET /bundle/summary` also carries `retry` (`{running, paused, pause_reason, allowance_used_up, total, done, failed}`, or null), counted by the server (`repo.retry_progress`) from the latest `retry_unread_pages` job and the pages that run took (`page_analysis.retry_job_id`, set by `reset_unread`, migration 0132): done = entries read since the job was queued, failed = failed again since, total adds the pages still waiting. Pages the run did not take, copies, set-aside and word-less pages are never counted. `running` is true while the job is queued or running, or handed back (`resuming`) with a queue row still waiting for or held by a worker (`app.job_waits_in_queue`; a hold placed before the worker picked it up leaves `resuming` with no row, which is not running); `paused` while it is held back (e.g. the cost guard): S08 and the transcript's preparing screen then say "Trying again has paused for now. Please check back in a little while." and stop asking. A pause that is not resumable (the cost guard, the key service unreachable) never ends by itself, so it is not a dead end: `retry.pause_reason` (`cost_guard` or `other`, a fixed code, never text) picks the line ("This case has used up its processing allowance for now." or the general one), S08 and the transcript's preparing screen keep "Try these pages again" / "Try again" for people with `page.read` (but not while the case is still over its allowance: `retry.allowance_used_up`, computed from `app.ai_budget` and the cell's cost-guard settings, shows "This case has used up its processing allowance. Please contact support." (`bundle.retry_allowance_used_up`, `transcript.retry_allowance_used_up`) and no button), and a press hands the same paused job back to the queue (`record_entries.request_unread_retry` -> `app.resume_paused_job(job, queue)`, migration 0133: the queue must be `jobs`, the priority is the job's own and the queue row always takes a lock like 0035's; status stays `paused` with the short code `resuming` until the worker makes paused -> running; never a second job or queue row; refused while the case is on hold, and refused with 409 `allowance_used_up` while the case is over its allowance, `record_entries.case_over_allowance`; the screens show the same allowance line, also when a press meets that refusal). Every page was read." or "N pages still could not be read." plus the button; the transcript's preparing screen shows the same bar. The browser computes only the percent for display.
- Answers cut off at the output cap: raising the cap is the main fix (`ANTHROPIC_LIMITS.max_output_tokens` 32000, the direct client always streams; Bedrock fallback stays 16000; direct concurrency is 50 so concurrency x cap stays 1.6M of reserved output tokens). ## Worker connections and stuck jobs (production fixes)

- Pools: the worker's own pool (`WORKER_DB_POOL_MAX`, default 10) and the queue library's (`WORKER_QUEUE_POOL_MAX`, default 4) add up to less than a session pooler's pool size (15 by default; F51). Model calls in flight (`service._extract_concurrency`) are the provider's limit, at most the worker pool less `POOL_RESERVE` (2), at least one: by default only about 8 model calls run at once, on 10 + 4 pooler connections per worker replica. A second replica, or any other client on the same pooler role, needs more pooler capacity: raise the Supabase pool size first, then `WORKER_DB_POOL_MAX` / `WORKER_QUEUE_POOL_MAX` (`docs/runbooks/railway.md`).
- Dropped connection (F52): `JobWorker._persist` tries a job's status write again on a fresh connection (`DEFAULT_PERSIST_WAITS`); `JobWorker.requeue_orphaned()` (worker start and each housekeeping round; `app.requeue_orphaned_jobs`, migration 0134) puts back a `running` job whose queue row is no longer todo/doing, within `max_attempts`. The put-back writes name the status they move from, so a retry after a commit that went through is a no-op, and the sweep (migration 0135) also gives a `queued` job with no todo/doing row a new queue row, never one tagged `case_on_hold`.

## Page-batch jobs (scalability PR 17: design; build is a later PR)

Status: design for review. ADR `docs/decisions/0038-page-batch-jobs.md`. Today one `process_bundle` job (kind `process_bundle` or `process_pages`) holds the lock `case:<id>` and one worker slot for the whole bundle (split, ocr, sort, duplicates, missing, embed, ready), so the expert's own `rerun_checks` and `build_report` wait for hours and one 4,000-page bundle holds a slot for 75 minutes or more. Goal: read the pages in about 100-page child jobs on the `bulk` queue that take no case lock, keep the shared-state steps under the lock, and leave the browser, the API and the SSE stream exactly as they are.

### Switch and numbers (scalability rule 9: the default equals today)
- `PIPELINE_BATCH_PAGES` (default `0` = off, today's single job, unchanged; the recommended value once PR 18 has measured is `100`). `PIPELINE_BATCH_WINDOW` (default `6`): child jobs of one run queued or running at once. `PIPELINE_BATCH_MIN_PAGES` (default = `PIPELINE_BATCH_PAGES`): a run with fewer pages to read (an append of 14 pages, a small bundle) stays in one job as today. All three are read through `app/core/config.py` (foundation adds them). A run already planned is finished the way it was planned, whatever the flag says later.
- Child kind `read_page_batch`, `interactive=False`, `changes_case=False` (no lock), queue `bulk` (`registry.queue_for`), priority 0 like its parent. It is registered in `features/pipeline/__init__.py`, listed in the `api/AGENTS.md` job table and architecture section 7 (foundation-owned files: ask), and mapped to action `case.upload` in `app.job_kind_actions` like its parent.

### What takes the case lock and what does not
| Step | Runs in | Lock `case:<id>` | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| split (pikepdf, render, numbering) and plan | parent, first invocation | yes | assigns `bundle_page_no` (never changes) and chooses batches; two plans of one case must not overlap |
| ocr (T-07a, born-digital words) | child | no | writes only that page's `ocr_words` and `pages.text_source` |
| sort = extract (T-07b) | child | no | writes only that page's `page_analysis`, `record_entries` and `suggested` facts |
| embed | child | no | one vector per page; `pages_to_embed` has no copy filter, so doing it earlier changes nothing |
| wrong-person suggestion (`_suggest_quarantine`) | parent tail | yes | looks across the whole bundle after every page is extracted |
| duplicates (hash, compare, set aside) | parent tail | yes | compares pages with each other. Hashing stays here in this PR (a later optimisation may hash in the child; `pages_to_hash` then finds nothing left to do) |
| gaps and missing documents (T-07c) | parent tail | yes | needs every page's references and dates to match against the inventory |
| ready (activate case, notification) | parent tail | yes | changes `cases.status` |

So the lock is held only while the parent is actually running: the first invocation (split, plan) and the tail. While the children run the parent holds nothing, and `rerun_checks`, `build_report`, `export_case` and every other case job go ahead. The tail is the longest locked part; PR 18 measures it, and a second wave (T-07c as batch jobs on the same machinery) is the follow-up if it is more than about 10 minutes at 4,000 pages. Children writing pages without the lock is safe because (1) their page ranges are disjoint, (2) the data they write is read by no other job before the case is ready or the expert has read the page (checks and reports use verified facts only, rules 2 and 3), and (3) the API already wrote pages (quarantine, replace, rotate) outside the lock before this change, and each store checks `not quarantined` at write time (tested below). Kinds that relied on the lock to wait for the bundle (`reprocess_case`, `retry_unread_pages`, `extract_entries`, `repair_fact_dates`) pause with the resumable code `busy_processing` while the case has an open run, so they wait out the run with the existing back-off (F5) and then go; nothing is queued twice.

### Shape: one `jobs` row for the person, child jobs for the work
The parent stays the single `jobs` row that `GET /process`, `/bundle/summary`, retry, cancel and screen 07 already follow. Children are other `jobs` rows of kind `read_page_batch` that those routes never list (`BUNDLE_JOBS` and `latest_processing` name kinds). No job status is added.

1. **First invocation** (lock held): `ctx.step("split")`, then `ctx.step("plan")`. `plan` is one transaction: it reads `pages_without_words` and `pages_to_extract` for the case (reused unchanged, with optional `first`/`last` page-number arguments), drops pages inside a batch of another open run, and cuts the rest into batches of `PIPELINE_BATCH_PAGES` waiting pages by `bundle_page_no`. It inserts one `pipeline_runs` row (`state = waiting`) and the `pipeline_batches` rows, and queues the first `PIPELINE_BATCH_WINDOW` children with `app.enqueue_job` (so the access check and the `jobs` row are the usual ones). Rows, children and the checkpoint commit together or not at all (F6). Fewer pages than `PIPELINE_BATCH_MIN_PAGES`, or the flag off: no run is made and the handler reads the pages inline, today's code path unchanged.
2. The handler then **parks**: it raises `JobParked` (new, foundation). The worker leaves the row `running`, writes nothing, and the queue row ends; the slot and the lock are free. A `running` row with no queue row looks like an orphan (F52), so the orphan sweep skips a parent whose run is `waiting` (below).
3. **Child** `read_page_batch(batch_id)`: loads its batch (`first_page_no`, `last_page_no`, parent id), then `ctx.step("ocr")` -> `_read_words`, `ctx.step("sort")` -> `_extract`, `ctx.step("embed")` -> `_embed`, each over the pages of its range. The per-page checkpoints (`text_source`, `a.extracted_at`, the embedding row) are the progress, so a retried or resumed child redoes only the missing pages; `job_steps` only skips whole steps that finished. Cancel is read from the **parent** (`repo.cancel_requested(conn, parent_id)`), so a stop ends the child at a page boundary with `ProcessingPaused("cancelled")` as today. Its last transaction calls `app.pipeline_complete_batch(batch_id)` (fan-in, below).
4. **Tail** (the parent's second invocation): runs when the run is `ready`. It claims it (`ready -> finishing`, one statement), then runs `_suggest_quarantine`, `duplicates`, `missing` and `_ready` with the same `ctx.step` checkpoints and `steplog` lines, and ends `done` at pct 100. A crash in the tail retries the same row and skips finished steps (the run is `finishing`). The run becomes `done` in `_ready`'s transaction.

Handler entry, in order: no run, or run `done` -> today's path; `ready` or `finishing` -> tail; `waiting` -> `app.pipeline_reopen_run` (below), then park, or go straight to the tail if nothing is open. A stale queue row for a job already `done` does nothing (worker change below), so no path runs the tail twice; the lock also serialises two rows of one job.

### Fan-in: exactly once, crash-safe
`app.pipeline_complete_batch(p_batch)` (SECURITY DEFINER, `set search_path = ''` apart from the queue schema it defers into, worker role, refuses unless `app.current_job_case_id()` equals the batch's case) runs in the child's last transaction:
1. `select ... from pipeline_runs where job_id = <run> for update`. This lock is what makes the last two children safe: under read committed each would otherwise see the other's batch still open and neither would fire; with the row lock the second waits and then sees the first's commit.
2. `update pipeline_batches set completed_at = coalesce(completed_at, now())` (repeat-safe).
3. Top up the window: while the children queued or running are fewer than `PIPELINE_BATCH_WINDOW` and a batch has no child yet, queue its child with `app.enqueue_job`. One 40-batch bundle then cannot queue in front of every other bundle (round-robin by completion), and the queue holds at most window x runs children.
4. If no batch of the run is open, `state = 'waiting'`, the parent `jobs` row is `running`, and no *older* run of the case is still `waiting` or `ready`: `update pipeline_runs set state = 'ready' where state = 'waiting'` and, only if that changed a row, defer one `run_job` for the parent (queue and priority copied from the parent's latest queue row, lock `case:<id>`; the same construction as `app.resume_paused_job`). The state change and the queue row are one transaction, so a crash leaves both or neither, and a second caller finds the state already `ready`. Returns `fired` or `waiting`.

Runs of one case finish in the order they were planned: a younger run's tail (duplicates, missing) must see the older run's pages, or a reference to a page still being read would be listed as missing. When a tail ends, `app.pipeline_fire_next(case)` (inside `_ready`) runs step 4 for the case's next run.

`app.pipeline_heal_runs()` (worker sweep, below) repeats steps 3 and 4 for every `waiting` run (rows taken `for update skip locked`), so a fire or a top-up lost between two commits is made good within one sweep round (about 30 s).

### Sweep, pause, failure and resume
Foundation adds `JobRegistry.sweep(name)`: sweeps that features register run in `JobWorker._housekeeping` next to `requeue_stalled` and `requeue_orphaned`; a failing sweep logs its exception type only. The pipeline registers `app.pipeline_heal_runs()` which, for each open run:
- heals a lost fire or top-up (above);
- **mirrors the children onto the parent** (the person only ever sees the parent). Any child `failed` (dead-lettered after its attempts, or still paused after the 2-hour back-off) -> parent running -> failed with the child's code (`job_error`, `service_busy`, ...), the state "Try again" (`POST /process/retry`, `app.requeue_failed_job`) already handles. Any child `paused` with a code other than `resuming` -> parent running -> paused with that code (`cost_guard`, `cancelled`, `service_busy`, `model_unavailable`). When no child is paused any more and the parent's pause code was resumable (F5), parent paused -> running, without counting an attempt. `resuming` (a stopping worker's pause, F6) never touches the parent. These are transitions `state-machines.md` already allows.
- The orphan sweep `app.requeue_orphaned_jobs` (0134/0135) is replaced to skip a parent whose run is `waiting`, and a requeued job keeps the lock it had even when that is none (it reads the latest queue row whatever its lock, falling back to `case:` only when no row exists), so a child put back after a crash does not take the case lock. (`app.resume_paused_job` and `app.support_job_action` keep their `case:` fallback: a person resumes the parent, and `pipeline_reopen_run` builds children's rows itself.)
- On **Try again or resume** the parent handler enters `app.pipeline_reopen_run(run)` (SECURITY DEFINER, worker role, idempotent): children `failed` -> `queued` with attempts 0 and a new queue row; children `paused` -> a queue row brought forward or deferred as `app.resume_paused_job` does; batches with no child yet are queued up to the window; children still queued or running are left alone; the run stays `waiting`. Finished batches are never re-run and unfinished ones redo only their missing pages, so Try again costs only what is left. Never a second child for one batch (`child_job_id` is set once; reopen reuses the row).

### Progress and SSE unchanged
Children report to the parent's row. Foundation adds `JobContext.progress(..., job_id=None, monotone=False)`: `job_id` names another job of the same case (the worker role already updates jobs of its own case; RLS still refuses another case's) and `monotone` makes the update `greatest(pct, new)` and, within one step, `greatest(step_done, new)`, so two children writing at once cannot move the bar back. A child takes its numbers from the database, never from its own memory: `repo.run_counts(conn, run)` = pages with words and pages extracted, over `pipeline_runs.total_pages`. The parent row shows step `ocr` with "N of total" until every page has its words, then step `sort`; pct = 30 + 25 x (words + extracted) / (2 x total), so the 30 -> 55 bands and the Preparing screen's step list (`STEPS`) are unchanged; after the tail steps the bar reaches 100 as today. A tick every 25 pages per child, as `_extract` does now. The `jobs` NOTIFY trigger (migration 0062) already pushes the parent's row change to open streams. Counts and step codes only (rule 6).

### Bounds on total concurrency (the merged limiters stay the authority)
Every Azure OCR call takes the account-wide slot (`kit.slot(..., "azure_ocr")`, PR 4) and every model call the Bedrock/Anthropic slot (PR 3), inside the child exactly as in the parent today, so N children cannot exceed the provider ceilings: more children only wait on the slots, they add no calls. Per process: a worker runs `WORKER_CONCURRENCY` jobs on a pool of `WORKER_DB_POOL_MAX` (10); each child caps its own in-flight pages at `max(1, (pool - POOL_RESERVE) // WORKER_CONCURRENCY)` (today 8 // 4 = 2 per child, 8 in all) instead of the whole pool, or four children would queue for connections (PR 9 keeps connections out of slow I/O, so waiting would be slow, not deadlocked). Window x live runs bounds the queued children; PR 2's metrics show the bulk queue's oldest age. The window is the fairness knob: a lone bundle uses at most `window` slots.
