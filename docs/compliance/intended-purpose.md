# Intended purpose and regulatory position

Status: draft for review by a UK medical device regulatory adviser before launch. Not legal advice.

## Intended purpose statement

Avodeck is software that helps a qualified medical expert witness organise and review copies of a person's medical records **for the purpose of preparing evidence in legal proceedings**.
- It numbers, sorts and indexes pages.
- It suggests facts with links to the exact words on the page, for the expert to check.
- It tracks the expert's reading.
- It assembles the expert's own written opinion into a report that meets court requirements.

Avodeck does **not**:
- diagnose, prevent, monitor, predict, treat or alleviate disease or injury in any person;
- recommend or influence any decision about a person's medical care;
- generate clinical or medico-legal opinions (the expert writes every opinion; the AI never does, rule 1);
- calculate scores, risks or impairment ratings used to decide care;
- connect to clinical systems used to treat patients.

The users are qualified clinicians acting as expert witnesses, and their legal instructors. The outputs are used by courts and lawyers, not by anyone providing care.

## Medical device position (UK)

- Under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002, software is a medical device when its manufacturer intends it for a medical purpose for individual patients, such as diagnosis, monitoring or treatment.
- Avodeck's purpose is evidential review of historical records for litigation. We therefore believe it is **not a medical device**, so UKCA marking and MHRA registration should not be required.
- **The risk is in feature drift.** Any of these would need this assessment re-done before release:
  - producing risk scores about a living patient's current care;
  - a "should have referred" judgement generated by AI;
  - impairment ratings that schemes use to decide benefits (slice K: assess before building);
  - use by treating clinicians for ongoing care.

## NHS clinical safety standards

- DCB0129 (manufacturers) and DCB0160 (deploying organisations) apply to health IT systems used in the direct care of patients in England.
- Avodeck is not used in direct care, so we believe these standards do not apply.
- If Avodeck is ever sold to NHS organisations for incident or mortality reviews, re-assess. A light-weight clinical risk log in the DCB0129 style would be good practice regardless.

## Other regimes to note

- **UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018:** special-category health data. Avodeck acts as processor for the expert (controller). DPIA, ICO registration, data processing agreement and sub-processor list are needed before real data.
- **Court rules on AI:** the NSW Supreme Court practice note (SC Gen 23) and similar guidance elsewhere. Avodeck's design (AI finds and cites, the expert writes) and the methodology appendix are how experts can show compliance. Keep the methodology appendix accurate.
- **EU AI Act:** not in scope while we only serve UK, Australian and Canadian users. Re-check before any EU launch.

## Guardrails in the product that keep this position true

1. Rule 1 (no AI opinion) is enforced by schema tests.
2. Marketing and in-app copy never claim Avodeck identifies negligence, missed diagnoses or breaches; it "finds and links the facts for you to check".
3. "Things to look at" checks are framed as reading prompts that the expert decides, never as conclusions.
4. Any new feature that touches the list above goes through a short regulatory check recorded as a decision record.
