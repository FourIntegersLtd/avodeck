Timeline redesign plan

The landing page looks calm because it shows 13 chosen entries over 8 months. A real bundle has thousands. The fix is to change what the lanes show at each distance, add a ribbon for the whole record, and open every entry in a side panel instead of a new screen.

Based on main @ 2210637 (21 Oct 2026)Prepared 22 Oct 2026Owner: understand-owner (D), with foundation-owner for one token

The verdict

Keep lanes. Change the board into overview, story and inspector.

No other chart type does the job better. Lanes by record type over time is the form every clinical timeline has settled on, from LifeLines (1996) to SystmOne's Patient Timeline that your GPs already know. Lanes are also how the packs are built.

What fails today is the layout. Dots sit in a fixed month grid with no words. Each lane repeats every entry in an open list underneath. The Activity tab is a second, weaker overview that competes with the first.

The research supports one structure for this kind of work: an overview with a movable window, a labelled story board that drops to ticks and counts as it fills, and a side panel that always shows the source words. The landing page already has the right look and click-to-page behaviour. The plan makes the real screen work like it at real volumes.

Why it is jam-packed now

I measured the synthetic Mrs A bundle in evals/…/expected/facts.json. The board is not cluttered by accident. It cannot hold this much.

Specific causes in the code:

  1. Every entry is drawn twice. Timeline.tsx:198 opens a full list under any lane with more than 4 entries, so the board doubles as a long list. This is also why the draw budget had to rise to 1.5 s in 5000dde.
  2. Words vanish at 5 entries. timelineLayout.ts labels at most 4 dots per lane, and none once a lane has more than 4. Most of the time the board shows bare dots.
  3. Same-day results pile up. Twenty results on one morning become 20 overlapping dots instead of "Blood test, 20 results".
  4. Repeat prescriptions are points. 669 prescription facts are mostly the same drug reissued. LifeLines drew these as a bar with ticks.
  5. Three views, two date controls and a hidden "Adjust" panel. Window presets, From/To fields, a view switch (Chronology, Activity, Map), a zoom switch and status pills sit in five rows before any record appears.
  6. The Activity tab repeats the Chronology list under a density bar. Its useful part, density over time, belongs above the board as a permanent overview.

What the research says

A research pass covered clinical timeline studies, litigation chronology tools and techniques for dense time data. The findings that shape this plan:

  • Timelines win for patterns and gaps, and lose for simple lookups. In Health Timeline (BMC MIDM 2019), psychiatrists found deeper insights with the timeline, but their first insight was slower (about 13 s against 7 s). So a list must be one tap away.
  • Clinicians distrust pictures that hide the words. Sultanum's MedStory and ChartWalk work found visual-only summaries "compromise trust". Notes must stay visible. This is the strongest reason for a side panel with the page text.
  • Overview with detail is the most consistently preferred technique. No alternative has proved better for understanding (Cockburn, Karlson and Bederson, ACM Computing Surveys). Fisheye distortion shows no benefit and distorts intervals. Intervals are the evidence here, so fisheye is ruled out.
  • Aggregation is the standard cure for overplotting. EventFlow cut a healthcare display from 2,700 to 492 marks (−80%). LifeLines2 grouped entries by year, month and day.
  • Story layers only work when curated. Knight Lab advises about 20 items for a narrative timeline. Our flags and question-linked entries fit that number. All 3,691 entries do not.
  • Legal chronology products put a source-linked list first. CaseMap, Opus 2, CaseFleet, Supio and Wisedocs all do this, with a visual timeline as a toggle or strip. That supports keeping a List mode.
  • "Result filed, no action" is common. About 7% of abnormal lab alerts lacked timely follow-up (Singh, 2009). Gaps should be computed and drawn, not left for the eye to find. Our findings already do the computing.

Most studies had 5 to 14 users, and none tested medico-legal experts. The legal tool evidence is vendor material. Several papers were read from abstracts only. Treat this as direction and confirm it with the usability test in phase 0.

How an Apple designer would approach it

Apple's interface guidelines rest on clarity, deference to content and depth. Their tools for dense time data (Photos years, months and days; Health highlights; Swift Charts' scrolling window and selection) translate into these rules:

The record is the interface

Remove chrome until the entries are the loudest thing. Five control rows become one bar.

Always know where you are

The ribbon always shows the whole record and where the lens sits, so an expert never loses their place in 29 years.

Detail follows distance

Far away you see shape. Closer, you see labelled entries. Close up, you read every one. The board changes on its own, with no zoom switch.

Direct, but never only direct

Dragging the lens is the fast way. Every drag also has a button, a click target and a key, because our users are not confident with a mouse.

Continuity, not page jumps

Opening an entry slides in a panel; the board stays put. Changing the period animates the axis so the eye can follow.

Highlight, don't hide

Filters fade what doesn't match instead of removing it. The count of everything else stays on screen.

The design, part by part

1. One header bar

Title, case range, a Board / List switch (two choices, down from three), the "As the GP saw it on…" switch and the "things to look at" pill, in one row as on the landing page. The pill opens S13. Status and question filters become a second row of pills: Everything · Checked 38 · Please check 9 · Things to look at 2 · Q1 · Q2 · Q3. Pills highlight: matching entries stay full strength and the rest fade to 30% ticks. A small "Hide the rest" link turns highlighting into filtering for anyone who wants it.

Removed: the zoom switch, the Activity tab, the scroll hint, the "Adjust" disclosure, the From/To fields shown all the time and the per-lane open lists.

2. The ribbon (replaces the Activity tab and the date fields)

  • A full-width strip, about 110 px high, always covering the whole record. One thin row per lane (12 px) shows a density bar per month in the lane colour.
  • Pins above it: amber rings for things to look at, dashed rings for please-check entries linked to a question. These are the curated story points and are always visible.
  • The lens is a framed window with two handles. Drag it to move, drag a handle to resize, or click anywhere to centre it there. The board follows on release with a 250 ms axis animation. Keyboard: arrow keys move by a month, Shift with an arrow resizes, and aria-valuetext reads "From 1 February 2024 to 30 September 2024".
  • Preset pills beside it: The period in question (the default, from periodDefaultDates), Whole record, Last 2 years, and Choose dates…, which opens the two date fields in a popover. Year labels under the ribbon are buttons that set the lens to that year.
  • Following Cockburn's guidance, scrolling or opening entries on the board moves the lens but never changes the board. Only the expert's own action on the ribbon does that.

3. The story board: three automatic levels

The lens width sets the level. There is no zoom control.

Years · lens over 3 years

Each lane is a month-by-month density bar. Only pins for things to look at and question links carry labels. Click a bar to move the lens to that period.

Months · lens 4 to 36 months

Labelled cards for the most important entries. The rest become ticks, with a "+n" chip per busy week. This is the default with the period in question.

Days · lens under 4 months

Every entry gets a labelled card, stacked into up to 3 rows per lane. Same-day groups split into their parts on hover or in the inspector.

4. Layout rules for each lane (the core of the fix)

  1. Group first. Entries in the same lane on the same day become one card: "19 Feb · 6 results". The card takes the strongest status in the group (look > please check > checked) and the combined question chips. That alone cuts 3,691 marks to 1,614.
  2. Show repeated items as series. Repeat prescriptions of the same drug become one bar from first to last issue, with a tick per issue. This needs a series_key from the server (see the API section). Where there is none, entries stay as cards.
  3. Rank. Order each group by importance: thing to look at, linked to a question, please check, checked, then by date.
  4. Pack. Place cards in rank order at their date into the first of up to 3 rows (2 when 7 or more lanes have entries) where they fit. A card is about 150 px wide and needs a 12 px gap.
  5. Fold the rest. Anything that doesn't fit becomes a 2 px tick at its date. Ticks are counted per week (months level) or month (years level) into a "+n" chip. Clicking the chip opens the inspector as a list of those entries.
  6. Size lanes to content. A lane with one row is 52 px high and the busiest lanes get up to 3 rows. A lane with nothing in the lens collapses to a 36 px row reading "Nothing in this period". It is not hidden, because a gap is evidence.
  7. Keep the count honest. The footer always says "Showing 23 of 318 entries in this period. 295 are ticks; switch to List to read them all."

All of this is one pure function, layoutLane(groups, pxPerDay, rows) in timelineLayout.ts, tested without a browser. Only cards are DOM nodes. Ticks and density bars are drawn as one SVG path per lane, so a screen never holds more than about 300 nodes whatever the record size.

5. Gaps drawn from findings

Each finding in GET /findings already lists evidence_fact_ids. When a finding's evidence entries are both inside the lens, the board draws the landing page's dashed amber connector between them with the server's own wording ("85 days, no action recorded"). The browser does not work out what is a gap; it only draws what the checks found, which keeps rule 4. Following LezDo's distinction, the label says "no entry in the records" when the check found missing records rather than recorded inaction. That wording comes from the pack.

6. The inspector (opening any entry)

  • Clicking a card, chip, tick, list row or pin opens a 380 px panel on the right (a bottom sheet below 960 px). The board does not move. The selected card gets a green ring.
  • Contents, top to bottom: page number; kind and date; the summary; status and question badges; the page excerpt with the source words highlighted, as in the landing page's pop-up, from the existing page image and word boxes (pageImage.ts); the finding sentence if there is one; then two buttons, Open the full page (Read, S10) and Open what the GP saw on this day (Consultation, S12).
  • "Nearby" shows the rest of a same-day group and the entries just before and after. ‹ and › buttons and the ← → keys step through the timeline in date order, crossing lanes, and the lens follows if needed. Esc closes and returns focus to the card.
  • The selected entry goes in the address as ?entry=<fact_id>, so the back button and shared links reopen it. Coming back from S12 returns to the same lens and selection.

7. List mode (absorbs the Chronology tab)

The same lens and pills, shown as the existing ChronologyList, grouped under sticky month headings ("May 2024 · 6 entries"), with same-day groups folded into one row. Each row opens the same inspector, so the two modes differ only in the middle. List is the accessible equivalent of the board and the quickest path for lookups, as Health Timeline found. The window list renders only visible rows, so 3,691 rows stay fast.

8. Question highlight

Hovering or focusing a Q chip anywhere (pill, card or inspector) lights every entry linked to that question across all lanes and dims the rest. This gives the question-centred view from Bui's TimeLine without a second lane layout.

9. "As the GP saw it on…"

Keep the switch. When it is on, the board draws a vertical green rule at that day. Entries that arrived later stay faint to its right, and the server already removes them from the data. The ribbon shows the same rule.

Rules this plan keeps

  • Packs (rule 11): lanes, their order, colours, icons and every word still come from the pack and i18n keys. Series and gap wording come from the server. No lane is named in code; the fixture-pack test keeps passing.
  • No product logic in the browser (rule 4): grouping and packing are drawing only. Gaps, density counts and series come from FastAPI.
  • Blind read (rule 3): the inspector shows only what /timeline already returns for read pages. The page excerpt uses the same server-gated route as S12.
  • Accessibility: buttons and handles at least 52 px; body text at least 17 px; roving focus across cards (arrows move, Enter opens); the board is role="grid" with lane rows; List mode is a full text equivalent; motion stops under reduced motion; colour is never the only signal (shape and wording carry status).
  • Performance (P8): under 1 s for 1,500 entries, so the CI budget can go back from 1.5 s to 1 s. Measure 3,691 too.
One decision for foundation-owner. Card labels at 17 px fit about 4 cards per lane per 1,000 px. At 15 px for the summary line (date stays 17 px bold) they fit 6. Either way works; the 15 px option needs an exception to the 17 px body rule for chart labels, the same exception the landing page already makes.

Delivery plan

Each phase ships on its own behind timeline_v2, test-first under the tdd-feature skill, with verify-slice before merging.

Phase 0 · Prove it about 1 week

  • A clickable prototype on the Mrs A data, built from this page's mock-up and the real layoutLane.
  • Test with 5 GP experts on the current screen and the prototype. Tasks: find the abnormal result that was filed with no action; how many visits were there in June 1994; open the source page for the 15 May visit; what did Q2's entries have in common? Record time, success and wrong clicks.
  • Go on if the prototype is at least as fast on lookups and clearly faster on the gap task.

Phase 1 · Inspector and fewer controls about 1 week · lowest risk, quickest gain

  • New TimelineInspector.tsx, opened from today's board and Chronology, with keyboard stepping and ?entry=.
  • Remove the per-lane open lists (Timeline.tsx:198–207), the Activity tab, the zoom switch and the Adjust panel. Move status and question to pills.
  • Tests: opening from card, list and chip; Esc returns focus; ← → crosses lanes in date order; the address reopens the entry; axe passes.

Phase 2 · Ribbon and lens about 1.5 weeks

  • The API /timeline/overview endpoint with tests under fixture packs.
  • TimelineRibbon.tsx with lens drag, click and keyboard, presets, year buttons and pins. Delete ActivityDensityStrip.tsx and TimelineActivity.tsx; keep activityBuckets.ts logic only if the ribbon reuses it.
  • Tests: dragging commits on release; keys move and resize; presets set the right dates; the lens follows inspector stepping; touch targets are at least 52 px.

Phase 3 · Story board about 2 weeks

  • groupByDay, rankGroups and layoutLane in timelineLayout.ts; the three automatic levels; ticks and density as SVG paths; "+n" chips; lane sizing; the honest count.
  • Highlight-not-hide pills; question highlight; the "as seen on" rule.
  • Tests: the same day and lane makes one card with the strongest status; no two cards overlap; rank order decides who gets a label; folded counts add up to the lane total; a 1,500-entry draw is under 1 s and under 400 nodes.

Phase 4 · Series and gaps about 1.5 weeks, needs pack work

  • A pack rule for series (foundation-owner reviews the pack schema), series_key on events, and prescription bars.
  • Timeline.gaps from findings, and connectors drawn when both ends are in the lens.
  • Tests: the fixture pack's series rule changes the bars with no code change; a gap shows only when both entries are in view; the wording comes from the pack.

Phase 5 · Finish about 1 week

  • Animations (axis tween, panel slide) with reduced-motion fallbacks; bottom sheet below 960 px; dark mode check; canvas board S11 updated to match.
  • Re-run the phase 0 test. Remove the flag. Put the CI draw budget back to 1 s.

Success measures

  • The gap task is found within 60 s by at least 4 of 5 experts (today's baseline is set in phase 0).
  • Lookups are no slower than in today's Chronology view.
  • Draw time is under 1 s at 1,500 entries and under 1.5 s at 3,691, with fewer than 400 nodes on screen.
  • No support requests about "too many dots" (the trigger already named in spike S11).

Risks

  • Dragging is hard for some users. Every lens action also has a button, a click and a key. Presets are the default way in.
  • Ranking can hide a quiet entry that matters. The count line and List mode always show the total, highlighting never removes anything, and the expert's own question links raise an entry's rank.
  • Automatic levels can surprise. The level name ("Showing months") sits in the axis corner, and the change animates.
  • Series rules differ by specialty. They live in packs, and the fixture-pack test proves it.
