Data processing agreement

Effective date: 1 November 2026
Last updated: 1 November 2026

This agreement sets out how we handle the medical records you upload to Avodeck. It satisfies Article 28 of the UK General Data Protection Regulation. By creating an Avodeck account, you accept this agreement.

1. Who this is between

This agreement is between:

  • You (the "controller") — the expert witness or organisation that uploads medical records to Avodeck. You decide why and how those records are processed.
  • Four Integers Ltd (the "processor", "we", "us") — a company registered in England and Wales. Dr Aishat Abubakar is the director. We handle the records only to provide Avodeck to you.

2. What this covers

This agreement covers all personal data you upload to, create in, or cause to be processed through Avodeck.

What we do with it

  • Store your uploaded medical record bundles (encrypted)
  • Read each page with optical character recognition
  • Use AI to find facts in the records (dates, consultations, prescriptions, results), each linked to the exact words on a numbered page
  • Show you the pages and facts so you can check them
  • Put together your report from the facts you have verified and the opinions you have written
  • Help with later stages: written questions, joint expert meetings, trial bundles and the witness box
  • Delete everything securely when the time comes

  • Solicitors, colleagues and other people named in the case materials

3. Your instructions

We process personal data only on your instructions. Your instructions are:

  1. Process data as needed to provide Avodeck, as set out in the terms of service.
  2. Follow whatever you do in Avodeck (uploading a bundle, creating a case, requesting a deletion).

If we ever think an instruction would break data protection law, we will tell you straight away. If the law requires us to process data in a way you did not ask for, we will tell you before we do (unless the law says we cannot).

We will not use the records for anything else. We do not train AI models on your data, run analytics on patient records, or use them for marketing.

4. Confidentiality

Everyone who handles personal data on our side is bound by a duty of confidentiality, by contract or by law. Our support team cannot see the content of your cases unless you give them a time-limited grant (up to 24 hours), and every access is logged.

5. Security

We take security seriously. Here is a summary of what we do; the full technical details are in our privacy policy.

MeasureWhat it means
EncryptionEach case has its own encryption key. Even if someone broke into the database, they would see scrambled data. Patient names and dates of birth have an extra layer.
Access controlEvery request checks who you are and what you are allowed to do. The database repeats the check. Only the named expert and the people they add can see a case.
Two-step sign-inRequired for important actions like signing, serving, exporting or deleting.
Identifiers removed before AINames, NHS numbers and dates of birth are stripped from the text before it goes to the AI model. The AI provider does not keep your data and does not train on it.
Audit trailEvery action is logged in a tamper-resistant chain. Who did what, when, to which page.
Virus scanningEvery uploaded file is scanned before it is processed. If the scan fails, the file is rejected.

We regularly test our security and will carry out an independent penetration test at least once a year.

7. Helping with data subject rights

If a patient (or their representative) asks to see, correct or delete data in one of your cases, that request comes to you as the controller. We will help you respond by providing Avodeck's export and deletion features and, where needed, reasonable extra help.

8. If something goes wrong

If we discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you within 24 hours and give you:

  • What happened
  • What data was affected and roughly how much
  • What the likely consequences are
  • What we have done or plan to do about it

We will cooperate with you so you can meet your own duties to the ICO and any affected people.

9. Helping with impact assessments

If you need to carry out a data protection impact assessment for your use of Avodeck, we will give you the information you need. We maintain our own DPIA, which we can share on request.

10. Where data is kept

All patient data at rest is stored in the United Kingdom. The only processing outside the UK happens on Railway (Amsterdam, Netherlands), where data passes through container memory only and nothing is saved to disk. The Netherlands is in the EEA, which has adequate data protection standards.

We will not move patient data to any other country without telling you first and making sure there is an appropriate legal safeguard.

11. Deletion

When a case reaches the end of its retention period, or when you ask us to delete it, we will:

  1. Destroy the case's encryption key (cryptographic shredding), making all stored data unreadable
  2. Remove all rows from the database
  3. Send you a certificate of deletion confirming what was deleted, how many records, and the date by which backup copies will have expired

Before we delete anything, you can export your data through Avodeck. We will keep the export feature available for at least 30 days after you ask to leave.

We may keep pseudonymised audit metadata (what kind of event, when, an identifier that points to nothing) for as long as the law requires.

12. Audits

We will give you the information you need to confirm we are meeting our obligations under this agreement and the UK GDPR. On reasonable notice, we will allow you (or someone you appoint) to carry out an audit or inspection.

13. Changes

We may update this agreement to reflect changes in how we process data or in the law. We will give you at least 30 days' notice. If you keep using Avodeck after that, you accept the updated agreement.

14. Law

This agreement is governed by the laws of England and Wales. If there is a conflict between this agreement and the terms of service, this agreement wins on data protection matters.
